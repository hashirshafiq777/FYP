# Dhamki, harassment, ya zulm ho to kya karein?

## Immediate safety
If there is an immediate risk of injury, move to a safer place, contact trusted support and the local emergency/police service, and get medical help if needed. If the person is nearby, avoid confronting them alone.

## Preserve and report
1. Save the original messages, call logs, voice notes, photos, emails, dates/times, witness details, and any medical report. Back them up somewhere the other person cannot access. Do not edit or circulate sensitive material publicly.
2. Write a short factual timeline and make a written complaint to the relevant police station/authority. Ask for a receiving/diary number and keep a copy. For a cognizable offence, CrPC section 154 concerns recording information; what legal category applies depends on facts.
3. If authorities do not act, keep proof of your complaint and consult a local advocate about escalation to a senior officer, a competent court, or a relevant protection body. Do not make false allegations or threaten retaliation.
4. If the abuse is domestic, workplace, online, or against a child, say which one and provide city/province: special protection/harassment/cybercrime rules and reporting routes differ. Ask a lawyer or support service to plan safety and confidentiality.

PPC section 503 defines criminal intimidation and section 506 sets punishment in the statute; other laws may apply to harassment, domestic violence, cyber abuse, or child protection. A vague feeling of “zulm” needs a follow-up question about what happened so the app can identify the right route. In danger, safety steps come first.
