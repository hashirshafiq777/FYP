# FIR ho jaye to sab se pehle kya karein?

This is general guidance for Pakistan. Registration of an FIR starts the criminal process; it does not prove guilt. FIRs and bail procedure are governed mainly by the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898 (CrPC), the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC), and any special law named in the FIR. Ask which province/territory and city the matter is in because police procedures and applicable local amendments can differ.

## Immediate steps
1. Get a complete, readable copy of the FIR. Note the FIR number, date, police station, district, complainant, accused persons, and every section/statute written in it. Do not rely only on a verbal description or forwarded screenshot.
2. Contact a criminal advocate practising in that district promptly. Send the advocate the FIR and a short factual timeline. If the person has been arrested, say so immediately.
3. Preserve evidence that may help: original messages, call logs, location records, receipts, documents, CCTV details, and names of genuine witnesses. Keep originals and backups; do not edit, delete, fabricate, or post them publicly.
4. Do not threaten the complainant or witnesses, arrange a false counter-FIR, hide from lawful process, or sign blank or unread papers. Do not pay anyone claiming they can “settle” the case unofficially.
5. Follow the advocate's directions for appearing before police/court and keep copies/receipts for every submission.

## Bail: what to ask the advocate
“Bail” is a court release subject to conditions; it is not an acquittal. The exact FIR sections, alleged role, evidence, arrest status, and court jurisdiction matter. For bailable offences, CrPC section 496 generally addresses release on bail when its conditions are met. For non-bailable offences, section 497 sets the court's framework and exceptions. The High Court or Court of Session has bail powers under section 498; section 498A places limits on orders for people not in custody/present before court or where no case is registered. Do not assume bail is automatic or that every offence has the same route.

If arrest is feared, ask a local advocate urgently whether a pre-arrest bail application is legally available in the specific circumstances and which court should hear it. If already arrested, ask about post-arrest bail, production before the magistrate, and the next remand/hearing date. The advocate should verify the current consolidated law and local amendments.

## If police refuse to record a complaint or take action
Keep a dated copy of the written complaint and proof/receipt of delivery. Ask a local advocate about escalation to the relevant senior police officer or the competent court, including any CrPC remedy that applies. Do not make up allegations to force registration.
