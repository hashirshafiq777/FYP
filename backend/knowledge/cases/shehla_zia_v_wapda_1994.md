# Ms. Shehla Zia v. WAPDA

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1994 **Citation:** PLD 1994 SC 693

## Background / Facts
Residents of a residential sector in Islamabad, led by Shehla Zia and other women, learned that the Water and Power Development Authority (WAPDA) planned to construct a high-voltage grid station in a green belt close to their homes. They wrote to the Supreme Court expressing fears that the electromagnetic field from the grid station and transmission lines would be hazardous to the health of residents and that the construction would damage the environment and the planned character of the area. The letter was treated as a petition under Article 184(3), since it raised a question of public importance relating to fundamental rights. WAPDA argued that there was no conclusive scientific evidence that such fields harm human health and that the station was needed for the city's electricity supply. At that time Pakistan had no comprehensive environmental law in force, as the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act was passed only in 1997.

## Legal Issues
- Does the right to life in Article 9 of the Constitution include the right to a clean and healthy environment?
- Can the Supreme Court act under Article 184(3) on the basis of a letter and a potential, uncertain hazard rather than an established injury?
- How should a court deal with scientific uncertainty about environmental harm?

## Decision
The Supreme Court held that the word "life" in Article 9 must be given a wide meaning and includes all facilities and amenities that a person is entitled to enjoy with dignity, legally and constitutionally, which includes a healthy environment free from hazards. The Court also relied on Article 14 (dignity of man). It referred to the precautionary principle from the Rio Declaration of 1992, stating that where there is a threat of serious damage, lack of full scientific certainty should not be used as a reason to postpone measures to prevent harm. Rather than deciding the scientific question itself, the Court directed WAPDA not to proceed with construction until the matter was examined, appointed an expert body (NESPAK) as a commission to study the issue, and asked the authority to consider alternatives. The parties were encouraged to resolve the matter with scientific input.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 9, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (security of person, right to life)
- Article 14, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (inviolability of dignity of man)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973
- Rio Declaration on Environment and Development 1992, Principle 15 (precautionary principle), referred to as persuasive

## Why It Matters
Shehla Zia is the foundation of environmental jurisprudence in Pakistan. It established that the constitutional right to life includes the right to a healthy environment and introduced the precautionary principle into Pakistani law, years before the environmental protection statute was enacted. Later cases on climate change, such as Asghar Leghari v. Federation (Lahore High Court, 2015), and Supreme Court decisions on housing, water and pollution, build on it. It is also an example of the Supreme Court accepting a simple letter as a petition under Article 184(3).

Islamabad ke kuch rehaishi logon ne Supreme Court ko khat likha ke WAPDA un ke gharon ke paas grid station bana raha hai jo sehat ke liye khatarnak ho sakta hai. Court ne kaha ke jab nuqsan ka khatra ho to science ki poori yaqeen-dahani ka intezar nahi karna chahiye, aur WAPDA ko kaam rokne aur mahireen se raye lene ka hukm diya.
