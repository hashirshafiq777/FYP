# Mst. Asia Bibi v. The State

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2018 (judgment of 31 October 2018, reported in 2019) **Citation:** PLD 2019 SC 64

## Background / Facts
Asia Bibi (Aasiya Noreen), a Christian farm worker from the village of Ittan Wali in Sheikhupura district, Punjab, was accused in June 2009 of making derogatory remarks about the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) during a quarrel with Muslim women co-workers over drinking water from a shared vessel. An FIR was registered several days after the incident at the instance of a local prayer leader, based on a public gathering at which Asia Bibi was said to have confessed. In November 2010 a Sessions Court in Nankana Sahib convicted her under Section 295-C of the Pakistan Penal Code and sentenced her to death; she became the first woman sentenced to death for blasphemy in Pakistan. In October 2014 the Lahore High Court dismissed her appeal. Her case became a focus of international attention; Punjab Governor Salmaan Taseer and Federal Minister Shahbaz Bhatti, who had spoken in her support and called for reform of the blasphemy law, were both assassinated in 2011.

## Legal Issues
- Was the prosecution evidence, including the delayed FIR, the alleged public confession and the testimony of the complainant and the women co-workers, reliable enough to prove the offence beyond reasonable doubt?
- What standard of proof applies in blasphemy cases carrying the death penalty?
- Did material contradictions in the witnesses' statements entitle the accused to the benefit of doubt?

## Decision
On 31 October 2018 a three-member bench headed by Chief Justice Mian Saqib Nisar, with Justices Asif Saeed Khan Khosa and Mazhar Alam Khan Miankhel, unanimously allowed the appeal, set aside the conviction and death sentence and acquitted Asia Bibi, ordering her release. The Court found that the FIR was lodged after an unexplained delay of five days, that the alleged public confession was extracted in front of a hostile crowd and was not voluntary, and that the two main witnesses contradicted each other and the complainant on important facts. The judgment stressed that the prosecution had failed to prove its case beyond reasonable doubt and that a conviction cannot rest on such evidence. Justice Khosa's concurring note observed that making a false accusation of blasphemy is itself a serious wrong and that the complainant's conduct in this case had resembled a violation of the Islamic duty not to bear false witness. A review petition was dismissed on 29 January 2019, and Asia Bibi later left Pakistan.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 295-C, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (use of derogatory remarks in respect of the Holy Prophet; punishable with death or imprisonment for life, with death as the only sentence after Muhammad Ismail Qureshi v. Pakistan)
- Section 154, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (FIR; delay in registration)
- Articles 37 to 39, Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984 (confessions; a confession made under inducement, threat or promise is irrelevant)
- Section 342, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (statement of the accused)

## Why It Matters
This is the most prominent Supreme Court judgment on blasphemy and demonstrates that the ordinary rules of criminal evidence, especially the benefit of doubt, apply fully to Section 295-C cases. It is widely cited for the proposition that delayed FIRs, hostile-crowd confessions and contradictory witnesses cannot sustain a death sentence. The judgment and the violent protests that followed also showed the political sensitivity of blasphemy cases and the danger faced by judges, lawyers and accused persons.

## Simple Explanation (Roman Urdu)
Asia Bibi, ek Masihi mazdoor khatoon, par 2009 mein tauheen-e-risalat ka ilzam lagaya gaya aur 2010 mein unhen sazaye maut sunai gayi, jo Lahore High Court ne bhi barqarar rakhi. Supreme Court ne October 2018 mein unhen bari kar diya kyun ke FIR paanch din dair se darj hui thi, gawahon ke bayanat ek doosre se takrate thay, aur jo iqrar hujoom ke samne liya gaya tha woh qanooni tor par qabil-e-qabool nahi tha. Court ne kaha ke itne kamzor saboot par kisi ko sazaye maut nahi di ja sakti. Yeh faisla tauheen ke muqadmaat mein saboot ke usoolon ki sab se ahem misaal hai.
