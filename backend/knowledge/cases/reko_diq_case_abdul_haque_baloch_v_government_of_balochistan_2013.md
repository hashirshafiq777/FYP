# Maulana Abdul Haque Baloch v. Government of Balochistan (Reko Diq Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2013 (judgment of 7 January 2013) **Citation:** PLD 2013 SC 641

## Background / Facts
Reko Diq, in Chagai district of Balochistan, holds one of the largest undeveloped copper and gold deposits in the world. In 1993 the Balochistan Development Authority signed the Chagai Hills Exploration Joint Venture Agreement (CHEJVA) with BHP Minerals, giving BHP a 75 percent interest and the province 25 percent, and the Balochistan Mining Concession Rules 1970 were relaxed to accommodate it. BHP's interest was later transferred to Tethyan Copper Company (TCC), which came to be owned jointly by Antofagasta of Chile and Barrick Gold of Canada. TCC spent several hundred million dollars on exploration and feasibility studies and applied in 2011 for a mining lease, which the Balochistan government refused. Maulana Abdul Haque Baloch and others filed petitions under Article 184(3), alleging that the agreement had been made without transparency, in violation of mining law, and against the interests of the people of Balochistan. A three-member bench headed by Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry heard the case; the Court had earlier, in 2007, dismissed a similar petition, but the matter was reopened.

## Legal Issues
- Was CHEJVA validly executed, and did the Balochistan Development Authority have the power to enter into it?
- Was the relaxation of the Balochistan Mining Concession Rules 1970 lawful?
- Were the agreement and subsequent transfers to TCC consistent with the Mineral Development Act, the Contract Act and public policy?
- Could the Supreme Court review a commercial agreement under Article 184(3)?

## Decision
The Supreme Court declared CHEJVA and all related agreements, including the addendum and the option and novation agreements under which TCC acquired its interest, to be illegal, void and non-est. It held that the Balochistan Development Authority had no authority to enter into the agreement, that the relaxation of the mining rules was contrary to law, that the agreement was opposed to public policy, and that the process lacked transparency required of dealings with public property and natural resources. The Court held that the state's mineral resources are held in trust for the people and that executive authorities must act within the law. TCC then pursued arbitration against Pakistan before the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) and the International Chamber of Commerce. In July 2019 the ICSID tribunal awarded TCC approximately 5.8 billion US dollars in damages against Pakistan for breach of the Australia-Pakistan bilateral investment treaty. In 2022 Pakistan, Balochistan and Barrick Gold reached an out-of-court settlement reviving the project, and in December 2022 the Supreme Court, in a Presidential Reference under Article 186, gave the opinion that the new agreements were not contrary to its 2013 judgment or to law.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973
- Article 172, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (ownerless property and natural resources; mineral rights)
- Article 173, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (power of federation and provinces to acquire property and make contracts)
- Balochistan Mining Concession Rules 1970 (and their relaxation)
- Regulation of Mines and Oil-fields and Mineral Development (Government Control) Act 1948
- Balochistan Mineral Rules 2002
- Contract Act 1872, Section 23 (agreements opposed to public policy are void)
- Balochistan Development Authority Act 1974
- Article 186, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (advisory jurisdiction, used in the 2022 Reference)

## Why It Matters
The Reko Diq judgment is the leading Pakistani case on the principle that natural resources are held in public trust and that agreements concerning them must comply with the law and be transparent. At the same time, the 5.8 billion dollar ICSID award that followed made it the most expensive judgment in Pakistan's history and a leading example in debates about judicial intervention in international commercial agreements and the country's obligations under investment treaties. The 2022 settlement and advisory opinion show how the matter was ultimately resolved through negotiation.
