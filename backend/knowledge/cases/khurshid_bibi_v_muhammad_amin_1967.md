# Mst. Khurshid Bibi v. Muhammad Amin

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1967 **Citation:** PLD 1967 SC 97

## Background / Facts
Khurshid Bibi was married to Muhammad Amin. The marriage broke down, and she alleged that her husband had taken a second wife and treated her badly, that she could not live with him, and that she was prepared to return the dower (mahr) and other benefits in exchange for dissolution of the marriage by khula. The husband refused to divorce her. She sued for dissolution of marriage under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 and, in the alternative, for khula. The trial court granted her relief, but the matter went through the appellate courts with differing results, and the question whether a court could grant khula against the will of the husband reached the Supreme Court. The earlier Lahore High Court decision in Mst. Balqis Fatima v. Najm-ul-Ikram Qureshi (1959) had held that a court could do so, but the point had not yet been settled by the Supreme Court.

## Legal Issues
- Under Islamic law, does khula require the consent of the husband, or can a court (qazi) dissolve the marriage on the wife's application without his consent?
- What is the test a court must apply before granting khula?
- What consideration (return of dower or other benefit) must the wife give for khula?

## Decision
The Supreme Court, in a judgment by Justice S. A. Rahman with a full bench, held that under the Quran (Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 229) and the Sunnah, a Muslim wife is entitled to khula as of right if she satisfies the court that she cannot live with her husband within the limits prescribed by Allah, and that the husband's consent is not necessary. The Court relied on the hadith of the wife of Thabit bin Qais, where the Prophet (peace be upon him) directed the husband to accept the return of the garden given as dower and to divorce her. It held that the court must be satisfied that the spouses cannot live together in harmony and that a forced continuation of the marriage would mean that they would not observe the limits of Allah; it is not necessary for the wife to prove fault or cruelty on the husband's part. The wife must restore the dower or such benefit as the court considers fair. Khurshid Bibi's marriage was dissolved by khula.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939, Section 2 (grounds for dissolution of marriage), including the residuary clause recognising any other ground under Muslim law
- Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:229 (basis of khula), applied as Muslim personal law
- Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, Section 7 (notice of divorce), relevant to the effect of a decree of khula as talaq
- West Pakistan Family Courts Act 1964 (procedure of family courts, as subsequently applicable)

## Why It Matters
Khurshid Bibi is the leading authority in Pakistan on khula and settled that a wife can obtain judicial dissolution of her marriage without the husband's consent and without proving fault, provided the court is satisfied the marriage cannot work. It gave Muslim women an effective exit from unhappy marriages and forms the basis of thousands of family court decrees every year. The principle was later made easier to apply by the 2002 amendment to the Family Courts Act requiring the court to grant khula at the pre-trial stage if reconciliation fails.
