# Dr. Muhammad Aslam Khaki v. Senior Superintendent of Police (Operations), Rawalpindi (Transgender Rights Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2009 to 2012 (final judgment of 25 September 2012, reported in 2013) **Citation:** PLD 2013 SC 188

## Background / Facts
In 2009, after reports that police in Taxila, Rawalpindi district, had raided a wedding function, arrested and mistreated several members of the khawaja sira (transgender and intersex) community, Dr. Muhammad Aslam Khaki, an Islamic law scholar and advocate, filed a constitutional petition under Article 184(3) in the Supreme Court. The petition described the social exclusion of transgender persons in Pakistan: lack of recognised identity, exclusion from education and employment, absence from the national database and voter rolls, denial of inheritance, and exploitation by "gurus" and by police. The Court, headed by Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry, issued notices to the federal and provincial governments and to NADRA and the Election Commission, and passed a series of interim orders over three years, calling for surveys of the community, registration and welfare measures.

## Legal Issues
- Are transgender and intersex persons entitled to the same fundamental rights as other citizens under Articles 4, 9, 14 and 25 of the Constitution?
- Can the state require them to identify as either male or female on identity documents, or must a third gender category be recognised?
- What positive measures must the state take regarding registration, inheritance, education, employment, voting and protection from harassment?

## Decision
The Supreme Court held that transgender persons (referred to in the proceedings as eunuchs or khawaja siras) are citizens of Pakistan entitled to all the fundamental rights guaranteed by the Constitution, including the rights to life, dignity, equality and property, and that their marginalisation was a violation of those rights. In its orders between 2009 and 2012, the Court directed NADRA to register transgender persons and to issue computerised national identity cards recording a third gender category based on the person's self-identification and, where needed, medical assessment; directed the Election Commission to register them as voters; directed the provincial governments to conduct surveys, admit transgender children to schools, provide employment opportunities and social welfare support, and to ensure that they receive their share of inheritance under Islamic law; and directed the police to protect them from harassment and extortion. The Court observed that Islamic law and the Constitution both protect their rights and that their exclusion was based on prejudice, not law. The final judgment of 2012 consolidated these directions.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973
- Article 4, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right of individuals to be dealt with in accordance with law)
- Article 9, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to life)
- Article 14, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (dignity of man)
- Article 25, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (equality of citizens)
- Article 23 and Article 24, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to acquire and hold property; protection of property rights), relevant to inheritance
- National Database and Registration Authority Ordinance 2000 (registration and identity cards)
- Electoral Rolls Act 1974 and Representation of the People Act 1976 (voter registration), as then applicable
- Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2018 (enacted later, building on the judgment)

## Why It Matters
This case was the first recognition of a third gender and of transgender rights by a superior court in South Asia, preceding the Indian Supreme Court's NALSA judgment of 2014. It led directly to NADRA's third gender option on identity cards, voter registration of transgender persons and, eventually, the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2018, which recognised self-perceived gender identity and prohibited discrimination. The 2018 Act was later partly struck down by the Federal Shariat Court in 2023 on the question of self-identification, and that decision is under appeal, but the Supreme Court's 2009 to 2012 orders remain the foundation of transgender rights in Pakistan.

2009 mein Taxila mein police ne khawaja sira community ke logon ko shadi ki taqreeb se giraftar kar ke un ke saath bura sulook kiya. Dr. Aslam Khaki ne Supreme Court mein darkhwast di ke khawaja siraon ko shanakhti card, wirasat, taleem, rozgar aur vote jaise bunyadi haqooq nahi milte. Supreme Court ne kaha ke khawaja sira bhi Pakistan ke barabar ke shehri hain aur unhen aain ke tamam haqooq hasil hain. Court ne NADRA ko teesri jins (third gender) ke saath shanakhti card banane, Election Commission ko vote ka indraj karne aur hukumaton ko taleem, rozgar aur wirasat mein hissa yaqeeni banane ka hukm diya. Is faisle ki bunyad par 2018 mein transgender persons ka qanoon bana.
