# Sarfraz Shah Killing Case (The State v. Shahid Zafar and others, Karachi Rangers)

**Court:** Anti-Terrorism Court Karachi (trial, August 2011); Sindh High Court (appeal) **Year:** 2011 **Citation:** citation not confirmed

## Background / Facts
On 8 June 2011, Sarfraz Shah, an unarmed young man aged about 18 to 22, was shot by personnel of the Pakistan Rangers (Sindh) in a public park near Boat Basin in the Clifton area of Karachi. Rangers personnel claimed he was a robber who had been caught by the public. A video recorded by a television cameraman showed Shah pleading for his life while being dragged by the hair at gunpoint, and then being shot twice at close range by a Ranger and left bleeding on the ground without medical help; he died on the way to hospital. The footage was broadcast nationally and provoked outrage. The Chief Justice of Pakistan, Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry, took suo motu notice, and the Supreme Court ordered the removal of the Director General of Rangers in Sindh and the Sindh Inspector General of Police from their posts and directed a prompt investigation and trial. Six Rangers personnel and a civilian (Afsar Khan) were charged and tried before an Anti-Terrorism Court in Karachi.

## Legal Issues
- Whether the shooting of an unarmed, restrained person by law enforcement personnel was murder and not a lawful use of force.
- Whether the act created terror or insecurity in the public so as to constitute terrorism under the Anti-Terrorism Act 1997.
- What liability attached to the other personnel present who did not fire but took part in the incident and failed to provide help.

## Decision
On 12 August 2011 the Anti-Terrorism Court in Karachi, in a trial completed within about two months, convicted all seven accused. Shahid Zafar, the Ranger who fired the shots, was sentenced to death, and the other five Rangers personnel and the civilian Afsar Khan were sentenced to life imprisonment, with fines and compensation ordered for the victim's family. The court found that the video and medical evidence proved that Sarfraz Shah was unarmed and posed no threat, and that the killing in a public place in daylight spread fear among the public. On appeal, the Sindh High Court maintained the convictions but commuted Shahid Zafar's death sentence to life imprisonment (year of the appellate decision not confirmed). The case remains one of the few in which paramilitary personnel were convicted for an extrajudicial killing.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 302(b), Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (qatl-e-amd)
- Section 34, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (common intention)
- Section 7, Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (punishment for acts of terrorism)
- Section 6, Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (definition of terrorism)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (suo motu notice by the Supreme Court)
- Article 9, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to life)

## Why It Matters
The case is the leading recent example of accountability for an extrajudicial killing by security forces in Pakistan and shows the role of video evidence, media and suo motu jurisdiction in forcing a prosecution that might otherwise not have happened. It affirmed that law enforcement personnel are subject to the ordinary criminal law and that use of lethal force against a restrained, unarmed person is murder. It is often cited alongside the Naqeebullah Mehsud case (2018) in discussions of police and paramilitary accountability.
