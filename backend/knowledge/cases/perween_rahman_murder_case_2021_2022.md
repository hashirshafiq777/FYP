# Perween Rahman Murder Case (The State v. Rahim Swati and others)

**Court:** Anti-Terrorism Court Karachi (trial, December 2021); Sindh High Court (appeal, November 2022) **Year:** 2021 to 2022 **Citation:** citation not confirmed

## Background / Facts
Perween Rahman was an architect, urban planner and the director of the Orangi Pilot Project Research and Training Institute (OPP-RTI), a well-known community development organisation in Karachi. She had documented land grabbing, illegal water hydrants and encroachments on village and state land on the outskirts of Karachi, and had received threats. On 13 March 2013 she was shot dead by gunmen on motorcycles near Banaras Chowk on Manghopir Road, Orangi Town, while returning home from her office. Police initially claimed that a Taliban commander killed in an encounter the next day was responsible, but her family, led by her sister Aquila Ismail, rejected this and petitioned the Supreme Court, which ordered a fresh investigation through a joint investigation team. Over several years the investigation focused on land grabbing linked to the area, and five accused, including Rahim Swati, Amjad Hussain Khan, Ayaz Swati, Ahmed Khan alias Pappu Kashmiri and Imran Swati, were arrested and tried before an Anti-Terrorism Court in Karachi.

## Legal Issues
- Whether the prosecution proved that the accused planned and carried out the murder, including the identity of the shooters and the motive linked to land disputes.
- Whether the confessional statements and the testimony of the witnesses, recorded years after the event, were reliable.
- Whether the killing fell within the Anti-Terrorism Act so that the trial before an Anti-Terrorism Court was proper.

## Decision
In December 2021 the Anti-Terrorism Court convicted all five accused of murder and terrorism and sentenced them to life imprisonment (two concurrent life sentences each, according to press reports). The accused appealed to the Sindh High Court. On 9 November 2022 a two-member bench of the Sindh High Court set aside the convictions and acquitted all five accused, finding that the prosecution had failed to prove its case beyond reasonable doubt; the court was critical of the investigation, the long delays in recording evidence and the inconsistencies in the prosecution's account. Perween Rahman's family and the Sindh government filed appeals against the acquittals in the Supreme Court; the outcome of those appeals is not confirmed in this note.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 302, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (murder)
- Section 34, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (common intention)
- Section 109, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (abetment)
- Section 7, Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (punishment for acts of terrorism)
- Section 164, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (recording of confessions and statements before a magistrate)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (Supreme Court's intervention in the investigation)

## Why It Matters
The case is a leading example of violence against human rights defenders and activists who expose land mafias in Karachi, and of how weak investigation can defeat even a high-profile prosecution. The Supreme Court's intervention to order a fresh investigation after a flawed police version is often cited by civil society as necessary oversight. The acquittals on appeal renewed debate about witness protection, delays in anti-terrorism trials and the quality of police investigations in Sindh.
