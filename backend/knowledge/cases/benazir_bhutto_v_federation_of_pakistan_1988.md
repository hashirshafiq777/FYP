# Benazir Bhutto v. Federation of Pakistan

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1988 **Citation:** PLD 1988 SC 416

## Background / Facts
During the rule of General Zia-ul-Haq, the Political Parties Act 1962 was amended to require every political party to register with the Election Commission, to submit its accounts and to meet other conditions, failing which the party could not take part in elections. Benazir Bhutto, as co-chairperson of the Pakistan Peoples Party, filed a petition directly in the Supreme Court under Article 184(3), arguing that the registration requirement and related amendments violated the fundamental right to form and be a member of a political party under Article 17. The Federation objected that the petition was not maintainable because no specific individual's right had been infringed and because the matter should first go to a High Court. The case was heard by a full bench headed by Chief Justice Muhammad Haleem.

## Legal Issues
- Could a political party or its leader invoke the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction under Article 184(3) without first going to a High Court?
- How should "public importance" and "enforcement of fundamental rights" in Article 184(3) be interpreted?
- Were the amendments to the Political Parties Act 1962 requiring registration of parties consistent with Article 17 of the Constitution?

## Decision
The Supreme Court held the petition maintainable and gave a wide, liberal reading to Article 184(3), holding that the traditional rule of locus standi (that only an aggrieved person may sue) does not restrict the Court when a question of public importance relating to fundamental rights is involved. The Court struck down the provisions of the Political Parties Act that made registration a condition for contesting elections and gave the Election Commission power to refuse or cancel registration, holding that they imposed unreasonable restrictions on the right to form political parties under Article 17(2). The Court read Article 17 together with the Objectives Resolution and the principle that the state is to be run through chosen representatives. The petition was allowed, and the judgment paved the way for the party-based general elections of November 1988.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 17, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (freedom of association and right to form political parties)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court)
- Article 199, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (High Court writ jurisdiction, discussed in relation to 184(3))
- Political Parties Act 1962, registration provisions added by amendments during the Zia era (exact section numbers not confirmed)

## Why It Matters
This judgment is the foundation of public interest litigation in Pakistan. By relaxing the rule of standing and reading Article 184(3) broadly, it allowed the Supreme Court to take up matters of public importance directly, a power later used in Shehla Zia, Darshan Masih and many suo motu cases. It also confirmed that the right to form a political party includes the right to contest elections, and that the state cannot use registration rules to control which parties may participate in politics.

Zia-ul-Haq ke daur mein qanoon banaya gaya ke har siyasi jamaat ko Election Commission mein register hona zaroori hai warna woh election nahi lar sakti. Benazir Bhutto ne is qanoon ko Supreme Court mein challenge kiya.
