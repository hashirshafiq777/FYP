# Malik Muhammad Mumtaz Qadri v. The State (Salmaan Taseer Murder Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2015 (judgment of 7 October 2015, reported in 2016) **Citation:** PLD 2016 SC 17

## Background / Facts
On 4 January 2011, Salmaan Taseer, the Governor of Punjab, was shot dead in Kohsar Market, Islamabad, by Malik Mumtaz Hussain Qadri, a constable of the Punjab Elite Force who was part of his security detail. Qadri fired more than twenty rounds and then surrendered to other guards. He stated that he killed the Governor because Taseer had publicly criticised the blasphemy law and had supported Asia Bibi, calling the law a "black law", which Qadri regarded as blasphemy. Qadri was treated as a hero by some religious groups and was showered with rose petals by lawyers at his first court appearance. On 1 October 2011 an Anti-Terrorism Court in Rawalpindi convicted him of murder and terrorism and sentenced him to death on both counts. In March 2015 the Islamabad High Court upheld the murder conviction and death sentence under Section 302 but set aside the conviction under the Anti-Terrorism Act, holding that the act was not terrorism. Both Qadri and the State appealed to the Supreme Court.

## Legal Issues
- Could criticism of the blasphemy law, or calling for its amendment, itself amount to blasphemy so as to provide a defence or mitigation for the killing?
- Can a private individual take the law into his own hands and kill a person he believes has committed blasphemy?
- Did the killing of a provincial Governor by his own guard, on the stated motive, fall within the definition of terrorism under the Anti-Terrorism Act 1997?

## Decision
On 7 October 2015 a three-member bench comprising Justices Asif Saeed Khan Khosa, Dost Muhammad Khan and Mazhar Alam Khan Miankhel unanimously dismissed Qadri's appeal and allowed the State's appeal, restoring the conviction and death sentence under Section 7 of the Anti-Terrorism Act in addition to the death sentence for murder. The Court held that criticising a man-made law such as Section 295-C, or calling for its reform, is not blasphemy and that the Governor had committed no offence. It held that no individual has the right to act as complainant, judge and executioner, and that the plea of "grave and sudden provocation" was not available because Qadri acted on reports of remarks made days earlier and had planned the killing. The Court found that the killing was designed to create fear and insecurity in society and to deter others from expressing opinions on the law, which satisfied the definition of terrorism. A review petition was dismissed in December 2015. Qadri was hanged in Adiala Jail, Rawalpindi, on 29 February 2016, and his funeral drew a very large crowd.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 302(b), Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (qatl-e-amd)
- Section 295-C, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (discussed in relation to whether criticism of the law is blasphemy)
- Section 6, Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (definition of terrorism)
- Section 7, Anti-Terrorism Act 1997 (punishment for acts of terrorism)
- Section 302(c), Pakistan Penal Code 1860 and the concept of grave and sudden provocation (rejected as a defence)
- Article 10A, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (fair trial) was referred to in the proceedings

## Why It Matters
The judgment is the Supreme Court's clearest statement that vigilante killing in the name of religion is murder and may also be terrorism, and that debate over the blasphemy law is lawful expression protected by the Constitution. It rejected the idea that a belief, however sincere, that someone has committed blasphemy can justify or mitigate murder. The case is regularly cited in later mob-violence and vigilante cases, including the Mashal Khan lynching case (2017).

## Simple Explanation (Roman Urdu)
Punjab ke Governor Salmaan Taseer ko 2011 mein un ke apne security guard Mumtaz Qadri ne is liye qatal kar diya ke Taseer ne tauheen-e-risalat ke qanoon par tanqeed ki thi. Supreme Court ne 2015 mein kaha ke kisi qanoon par tanqeed karna ya us mein tabdeeli ka mutalba karna tauheen nahi hai, aur koi shakhs khud judge ban kar kisi ko qatal nahi kar sakta. Court ne Qadri ki sazaye maut barqarar rakhi aur yeh bhi kaha ke yeh dehshatgardi ka jurm tha. Qadri ko February 2016 mein phansi di gayi.
