# Mian Muhammad Nawaz Sharif v. President of Pakistan

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1993 **Citation:** PLD 1993 SC 473

## Background / Facts
In 1993 a serious rift developed between Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif and President Ghulam Ishaq Khan over the President's powers and the appointment of the Army Chief. On 18 April 1993, the President dissolved the National Assembly and dismissed the Prime Minister and his cabinet under Article 58(2)(b) of the Constitution, which had been inserted by the Eighth Amendment and allowed the President to dissolve the Assembly if a situation had arisen in which the government could not be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. The dissolution order listed allegations of corruption, maladministration, subversion of the Constitution and a threat to the integrity of the armed forces. Nawaz Sharif challenged the dissolution directly in the Supreme Court under Article 184(3). Earlier, the Supreme Court had upheld the 1990 dissolution of Benazir Bhutto's government under the same Article.

## Legal Issues
- Were the grounds stated in the President's dissolution order sufficient under Article 58(2)(b)?
- Could the Supreme Court review the President's satisfaction that government could not be carried on in accordance with the Constitution?
- What relief could be granted if the dissolution was found unlawful, and could the Assembly and government be restored?

## Decision
An eleven-member bench headed by Chief Justice Nasim Hasan Shah held, by a majority of ten to one, that the dissolution order was unconstitutional and without lawful authority. The Court found that the grounds listed by the President were vague and did not show a breakdown of constitutional machinery of the kind required by Article 58(2)(b); mere differences between the President and Prime Minister were not enough. The Court restored the National Assembly, the Prime Minister and his cabinet. Justice Sajjad Ali Shah dissented. This was the first time the Supreme Court restored a dissolved assembly. However, the political deadlock continued, and in July 1993 both the President and the Prime Minister resigned under an arrangement brokered by the Army Chief, leading to fresh elections.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 58(2)(b), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (as inserted by the Eighth Amendment 1985; power of the President to dissolve the National Assembly)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973
- Article 48, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (President to act on advice)
- Article 91, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (Prime Minister and cabinet)

## Why It Matters
The case established that the President's power under Article 58(2)(b) was not unlimited and that the courts could examine whether the stated grounds really showed that government could not be carried on under the Constitution. It was the first judgment to actually restore an elected government after dissolution. The repeated use of 58(2)(b) in 1988, 1990, 1993 and 1996 eventually led Parliament to remove it through the Thirteenth Amendment (1997); it was restored by the Seventeenth Amendment (2003) and finally removed by the Eighteenth Amendment (2010).
