# Safia Bibi v. The State (Zina Ordinance Case)

**Court:** Federal Shariat Court **Year:** 1983 (decision of December 1983; reported later) **Citation:** PLD 1985 FSC 120 (not confirmed)

## Background / Facts
Safia Bibi was a nearly blind teenage girl (reported as between 13 and 16 years old) from a village in Sahiwal district, Punjab, who worked as a domestic servant. She alleged that she was raped by her employer and his son and became pregnant as a result; the child born to her died soon after birth. Her father lodged a complaint of rape (zina-bil-jabr) against the two men. The trial court acquitted the accused for lack of evidence, as there were no eyewitnesses and she could not identify the attackers with certainty because of her blindness. However, the same court used her pregnancy as proof that she had had sexual intercourse outside marriage, and in 1983 convicted her of zina (fornication) under the Offence of Zina (Enforcement of Hudood) Ordinance 1979, sentencing her to three years' imprisonment, fifteen lashes and a fine. The sentence caused national and international protests, including from the Women's Action Forum, and became a symbol of the injustice of the Hudood laws toward rape victims.

## Legal Issues
- Can an unmarried woman's pregnancy, by itself, be treated as proof of zina when she states that she was raped?
- What is the burden of proof in a zina case, and does an allegation of rape that fails for lack of evidence automatically convert into a confession of consensual zina by the woman?
- Was the conviction and sentence of a blind minor girl sustainable under the Ordinance?

## Decision
The Federal Shariat Court, in an appeal heard on priority after public outcry, set aside the conviction and acquitted Safia Bibi. The Court held that pregnancy of an unmarried woman is not by itself sufficient proof of zina, that a woman who complains of rape cannot be convicted of consensual zina merely because the accused men were acquitted, and that the trial court had wrongly used her own complaint against her. The Court observed that where a woman alleges rape, the benefit of doubt must go to her and that her failure to prove the rape does not establish her guilt. The judgment is usually associated with Chief Justice Aftab Hussain of the Federal Shariat Court (authorship not confirmed).

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Offence of Zina (Enforcement of Hudood) Ordinance 1979, Section 4 (definition of zina) and Section 10 (zina liable to tazir)
- Offence of Zina (Enforcement of Hudood) Ordinance 1979, Section 8 (proof of zina liable to hadd) and Section 6 (zina-bil-jabr, rape)
- Article 203D, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (jurisdiction of the Federal Shariat Court)
- Article 203DD, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (revisional jurisdiction of the Federal Shariat Court in Hudood cases)

## Why It Matters
Safia Bibi's case became the best-known example of how the Zina Ordinance could turn a rape victim into an accused, and it energised the women's rights movement in Pakistan during the Zia era. The principle that pregnancy alone does not prove zina was followed in later Federal Shariat Court cases. The injustice highlighted by the case contributed, over two decades later, to the Protection of Women (Criminal Laws Amendment) Act 2006, which moved rape back to the Pakistan Penal Code and separated it from consensual zina.
