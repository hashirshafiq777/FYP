# Sindh High Court Bar Association v. Federation of Pakistan (PCO Judges Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2009 **Citation:** PLD 2009 SC 879

## Background / Facts
On 3 November 2007, General Pervez Musharraf, as Chief of Army Staff, proclaimed a state of emergency, suspended the Constitution and issued the Provisional Constitution Order No. 1 of 2007 and the Oath of Office (Judges) Order 2007. Judges were required to take a fresh oath under the PCO; Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry and around sixty judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts refused or were not invited and were removed, many being confined to their homes. Shortly before the emergency, a seven-member bench of the Supreme Court had passed an order restraining judges from taking any oath under a PCO. New judges were appointed under the PCO, and in Tikka Iqbal Muhammad Khan v. General Pervez Musharraf (PLD 2008 SC 178) the PCO-era Supreme Court validated the emergency. After the lawyers' movement and the restoration of the deposed judges on 16 March 2009, the Sindh High Court Bar Association and Nadeem Ahmed filed petitions challenging the emergency, the PCO and the appointments of judges made under them.

## Legal Issues
- Were the Proclamation of Emergency of 3 November 2007, the PCO and the Oath of Office (Judges) Order 2007 constitutional?
- What was the status of judges who took oath under the PCO and of judges appointed during the emergency without consultation with the de jure Chief Justice?
- Were the Tikka Iqbal Muhammad Khan judgments valid, and what was the status of laws made during the emergency?

## Decision
On 31 July 2009 a fourteen-member bench headed by Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry unanimously declared the Proclamation of Emergency, the PCO and the Oath Order of November 2007 unconstitutional, void and without lawful authority. The Court held that Musharraf's actions were an unconstitutional act by an individual and could not be validated. The Tikka Iqbal Muhammad Khan judgments were declared to be without legal effect because they were delivered by a court that was itself unlawfully constituted. The Court held that judges appointed to the superior courts between 3 November 2007 and the restoration, without consultation with the constitutional Chief Justice, were not validly appointed and ceased to hold office, while the de jure judges who had refused the oath were held never to have been removed. Ordinances promulgated during the emergency were given temporary life so Parliament could consider them. The Court referred the conduct of judges who had taken the PCO oath despite the restraining order to the Supreme Judicial Council and to Parliament for further action.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Proclamation of Emergency of 3 November 2007
- Provisional Constitution Order No. 1 of 2007
- Oath of Office (Judges) Order 2007
- Article 6, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (high treason)
- Article 209, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (Supreme Judicial Council)
- Articles 177 and 193, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (appointment of judges)
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973
- Article 270AAA, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (inserted by the PCO regime to validate its acts, held invalid)

## Why It Matters
This judgment is the clearest rejection of the doctrine of necessity by the Supreme Court and the first time an emergency and PCO were struck down with full consequences for the judges who served under them. It followed the two-year lawyers' movement and marked a major shift toward judicial independence. The judgment also formed the basis of the later high treason trial of Pervez Musharraf under Article 6, which ended in a Special Court verdict in December 2019. The Eighteenth Amendment in 2010 added to Article 6 that an act of high treason cannot be validated by any court.
