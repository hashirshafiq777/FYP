# The State v. Dosso

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1958 **Citation:** PLD 1958 SC 533

## Background / Facts
Dosso and others were tribal residents of Balochistan who had been convicted of offences by a council of elders (jirga) under the Frontier Crimes Regulation 1901 (FCR). They challenged their convictions before the West Pakistan High Court, arguing that the FCR was inconsistent with the fundamental rights guaranteed by the 1956 Constitution, including equality before law. The High Court agreed and set aside the convictions. While the State's appeal was pending, on 7 October 1958 President Iskander Mirza abrogated the 1956 Constitution, dismissed the government and imposed martial law, with General Ayub Khan as Chief Martial Law Administrator. The Laws (Continuance in Force) Order 1958 was issued, under which the country was to be governed as nearly as possible by the old laws, but without the 1956 Constitution.

## Legal Issues
- Was the abrogation of the 1956 Constitution and the imposition of martial law legally valid?
- Were the fundamental rights of the 1956 Constitution still in force after the Laws (Continuance in Force) Order 1958?
- Could the FCR still be challenged as inconsistent with fundamental rights?

## Decision
The Supreme Court, led by Chief Justice Muhammad Munir, held that a successful revolution or coup d'etat is an internationally recognised method of changing a constitution, relying on Hans Kelsen's theory of revolutionary legality. Because the new regime was effective and its order was being obeyed, the Court said the 1958 takeover was lawful and the Laws (Continuance in Force) Order 1958 was the new legal order. Since the 1956 Constitution and its fundamental rights no longer existed, the FCR could not be struck down for violating them. The appeals were allowed and the convictions restored. Justice A. R. Cornelius dissented in part, holding that fundamental rights survived as part of the law of the land.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Frontier Crimes Regulation 1901
- Laws (Continuance in Force) Order 1958
- Constitution of Pakistan 1956 (abrogated), fundamental rights chapter including Article 5 (equality before law)

## Why It Matters
Dosso gave judicial validation to Pakistan's first military takeover and became the leading example of the "doctrine of necessity" and Kelsen's theory being used to legitimise a coup. It was expressly overruled in Asma Jilani v. Government of the Punjab (1972), where the Supreme Court declared that a usurper cannot be given legitimacy by the courts. The case is still taught as a warning about how legal theory can be used to justify unconstitutional power.

## Simple Explanation (Roman Urdu)
1958 mein Sadar Iskander Mirza ne 1956 ka aain khatam kar ke martial law laga diya. Isi dauran Dosso naami shakhs ka case Supreme Court mein tha jis mein FCR qanoon ko bunyadi haqooq ke khilaf kaha gaya tha. Supreme Court ne kaha ke jab koi inqilab ya takeover kamyab ho jaye to woh naya qanoon ban jata hai, is liye martial law jaiz hai aur purane aain ke bunyadi haqooq ab lagu nahi. Yeh faisla baad mein 1972 ke Asma Jilani case mein ghalat qarar diya gaya.
