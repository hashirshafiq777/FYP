# Zulfikar Ali Bhutto v. The State

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 1979 **Citation:** PLD 1979 SC 53 (appeal); review petition reported as PLD 1979 SC 741

## Background / Facts
On 11 November 1974, Nawab Muhammad Ahmad Khan was shot dead in Lahore when the car in which he was travelling with his son, Ahmed Raza Kasuri, was fired upon. Kasuri, a former PPP member who had become a vocal critic of Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, alleged that he was the real target. After the military takeover of July 1977, Bhutto was arrested and charged with conspiring with officers of the Federal Security Force (FSF), including its Director General Masood Mahmood, who became an approver (state witness), to murder Kasuri. Unusually, the trial was held directly before a full bench of the Lahore High Court rather than a sessions court, which removed one level of appeal. On 18 March 1978 the Lahore High Court convicted Bhutto and four FSF officials and sentenced them to death.

## Legal Issues
- Was the evidence of the approver Masood Mahmood and other FSF witnesses reliable enough to prove a conspiracy to murder?
- Was Bhutto's trial fair, given that it was held before the High Court under martial law?
- Should the death sentence be upheld or reduced?

## Decision
The Supreme Court heard the appeal for almost a year. On 6 February 1979 a seven-member bench, headed by Chief Justice Anwarul Haq, dismissed the appeal by a majority of four to three, upholding the conviction and death sentence. The three dissenting judges (including Justice Dorab Patel, Justice Muhammad Haleem and Justice Safdar Shah) would have acquitted Bhutto because the prosecution case rested on an approver's testimony and was not proved beyond reasonable doubt. The Court noted that the bench had originally been nine members but two judges left the bench before judgment (one retired and one fell ill). A review petition was dismissed on 24 March 1979, though the Court observed that the sentence of death could be reduced by the executive. President Zia-ul-Haq rejected clemency and Bhutto was hanged in Rawalpindi Central Jail on 4 April 1979. In March 2024, in Presidential Reference No. 1 of 2011, the Supreme Court gave an opinion that Bhutto had not received a fair trial under the Constitution.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 302, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (murder)
- Section 301, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (culpable homicide by causing death of a person other than the person intended)
- Section 120-B, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (criminal conspiracy)
- Section 109, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (abetment)
- Section 337, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (tender of pardon to an accomplice / approver)
- Article 186, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (advisory jurisdiction, used in the 2024 Reference)

## Why It Matters
The Bhutto case is the most controversial criminal trial in Pakistan's history and is widely described as a "judicial murder" by critics. It raised lasting questions about trials held under martial law, trials before the High Court as a court of first instance, and reliance on approver evidence. The 2024 Supreme Court opinion that the trial was unfair is a rare judicial acknowledgment of a historic wrong. The case is studied for the standard of proof in conspiracy cases and for the independence of the judiciary.
