# Noor Mukadam Murder Case (The State v. Zahir Zakir Jaffer)

**Court:** Sessions Court Islamabad (trial, February 2022); Islamabad High Court (appeal, March 2023); Supreme Court of Pakistan (appeal, May 2025) **Year:** 2022 **Citation:** citation not confirmed

## Background / Facts
Noor Mukadam, aged 27, was the daughter of Shaukat Mukadam, a former Pakistani ambassador. On 20 July 2021 she was murdered in the house of Zahir Zakir Jaffer, the son of a wealthy industrialist family, in Sector F-7/4, Islamabad. Evidence at trial showed that she had been held in the house against her will, had tried to escape (including jumping from a first-floor window), was prevented from leaving by the household staff, and was then tortured, raped and beheaded with a knife. Zahir Jaffer, who also held United States citizenship, was arrested at the scene. His parents, Zakir Jaffer and Asmat Adamjee, were charged with abetment and concealing evidence, the watchman and gardener with wrongful confinement, and employees of a counselling organisation (Therapy Works) with tampering with evidence. The case caused an outcry across Pakistan about violence against women and about the influence of wealthy accused.

## Legal Issues
- Whether the evidence, including CCTV footage, DNA, forensic reports and the accused's own conduct, proved the murder, rape and wrongful confinement beyond reasonable doubt.
- Whether the parents and household staff were liable as abettors or for concealing evidence and confining the victim.
- Whether the claim that the accused was mentally unfit to stand trial was genuine.

## Decision
The trial was held before Additional Sessions Judge Atta Rabbani in Islamabad. On 24 February 2022 the court convicted Zahir Jaffer and sentenced him to death for murder under Section 302(b), to 25 years' rigorous imprisonment for rape and to 10 years for abduction, with fines. The watchman Iftikhar and gardener Jan Muhammad were sentenced to 10 years' imprisonment each for wrongful confinement, while the parents and the Therapy Works employees were acquitted. On 13 March 2023 the Islamabad High Court dismissed Jaffer's appeal, upheld the death sentence for murder and enhanced the sentence for rape to death as well, while maintaining the sentences of the two employees. On 20 May 2025 a three-member bench of the Supreme Court upheld the death sentence for murder but converted the death sentence for rape into life imprisonment; according to press reports it also reduced the sentences of the two household employees (details not confirmed). The President rejected Jaffer's mercy petition later in 2025 (reported, not confirmed).

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 302(b), Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (qatl-e-amd)
- Section 376, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (rape)
- Section 364, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (kidnapping or abducting in order to murder)
- Section 342, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (wrongful confinement)
- Section 201, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (causing disappearance of evidence)
- Section 109, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (abetment)
- Section 34, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (common intention)
- Article 164, Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984 (admissibility of modern devices, relevant to CCTV and DNA evidence)
- Section 464, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (procedure where the accused is alleged to be of unsound mind)

## Why It Matters
The Noor Mukadam case is one of the most widely followed criminal trials in Pakistan in recent years and is often described as a test of whether wealth and influence can defeat justice. It demonstrated the effective use of CCTV, DNA and digital forensics in securing a conviction, and the trial court's refusal to accept delaying tactics such as repeated claims of mental illness. The case also drove public debate on femicide, "honour" and domestic violence, and strengthened demands for better protection of women in Islamabad and across the country.
