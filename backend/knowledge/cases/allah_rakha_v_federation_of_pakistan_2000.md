# Allah Rakha v. Federation of Pakistan (Muslim Family Laws Ordinance Case)

**Court:** Federal Shariat Court **Year:** 2000 (judgment of 1 January 2000) **Citation:** PLD 2000 FSC 1

## Background / Facts
The Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 (MFLO), promulgated by President Ayub Khan on the recommendations of a commission on marriage and family laws, introduced reforms such as compulsory registration of marriages, permission of an Arbitration Council for polygamy, a written notice procedure for talaq, and a rule that orphaned grandchildren inherit the share their deceased parent would have received. Religious scholars had opposed parts of the Ordinance since 1961 as contrary to classical Islamic law. For many years the Ordinance was protected from Shariat review, first because it was included in the Constitution's list of protected laws and later by the Supreme Court's decision in Federation of Pakistan v. Mst. Farishta (1981) that it was "Muslim personal law" outside the Federal Shariat Court's jurisdiction. After Parliament amended Article 203B in the 1990s to remove the exclusion of Muslim personal law, Allah Rakha and other petitioners filed Shariat petitions challenging several sections of the MFLO as repugnant to the injunctions of Islam.

## Legal Issues
- Did the Federal Shariat Court now have jurisdiction to examine the MFLO after the amendment to Article 203B?
- Is Section 4 (succession by orphaned grandchildren) repugnant to the Islamic law of inheritance?
- Are the talaq notice provisions of Section 7, under which a divorce does not take effect until ninety days after notice to the Union Council chairman, and the polygamy permission provisions of Section 6, repugnant to Islam?

## Decision
The Federal Shariat Court held that it had jurisdiction and declared several provisions of the MFLO repugnant to the injunctions of Islam. It held that Section 4 was repugnant, because under the Quranic law of inheritance a grandchild whose parent died before the grandparent is not an heir while nearer heirs exist, and that provision for such grandchildren should be made through wills or gifts instead. It also held that parts of Section 7 were repugnant, in particular sub-section (3), which makes talaq effective only after ninety days from the receipt of notice by the chairman, and sub-section (5), relating to the computation of the period during pregnancy, since under Islamic law a talaq pronounced in the proper form takes effect according to the iddat rules of the Shariah; a penalty for failing to give notice was however considered acceptable. Certain parts of Section 6 on polygamy and the related penalty provisions were also declared repugnant to the extent stated in the judgment. The Court directed the President to amend the law by a fixed date. The Federal Government appealed to the Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court, and because the appeal has remained pending, the judgment has not come into effect and the MFLO continues to be applied as enacted.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, Section 4 (succession by children of a predeceased son or daughter)
- Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, Section 6 (polygamy requires permission of the Arbitration Council)
- Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, Section 7 (talaq; notice to chairman; effectiveness after ninety days)
- Article 203B(c), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (definition of "law" for Shariat jurisdiction; former exclusion of Muslim personal law)
- Article 203D, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (power to declare laws repugnant to Islam)
- Article 203F, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (appeal to the Shariat Appellate Bench)

## Why It Matters
Allah Rakha is the most important challenge to Pakistan's main family law reform statute and illustrates the tension between the MFLO's modernising provisions and classical Islamic law. Because the appeal has been pending in the Supreme Court for more than two decades, the Ordinance remains in force, and courts continue to apply Section 4 and the Section 7 notice rules. Lawyers and students must be aware that the Federal Shariat Court's findings exist but are suspended by the pending appeal.

Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 mein kuch aise qawaneen hain jaise yateem potay-potiyon ko dada ki wirasat mein hissa dena aur talaq ko Union Council ko notice ke nabbay din baad muassar maanna.
