# Suo Motu Case No. 16 of 2011 (Karachi Law and Order / Karachi Unrest Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2011 (judgment of 6 October 2011) **Citation:** PLD 2011 SC 997

## Background / Facts
In 2011 Karachi experienced one of the worst waves of targeted killings in its history. Hundreds of people were killed in a few months in violence linked to political parties, ethnic tensions, land grabbing, extortion ("bhatta") and criminal gangs, with areas such as Lyari, Orangi, Qasba Colony and Kati Pahari becoming battlegrounds. In August 2011 Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry took suo motu notice under Article 184(3) of the breakdown of law and order and the failure of the state to protect the right to life. A five-member bench sat in Karachi for several weeks and heard the Sindh government, the police, the Rangers, intelligence agencies, bar associations and political parties, and received reports on the number of killings, the recovery of weapons, the role of "no-go areas" and the political affiliations of criminals.

## Legal Issues
- Whether the state and provincial authorities had failed in their constitutional duty to protect the life and property of citizens in Karachi under Articles 9 and 14.
- Whether political parties maintained armed or militant wings and whether the police had been politicised through unlawful appointments and transfers.
- What directions the Court could issue to the executive to restore order while staying within its judicial role.

## Decision
The Court held that the violence in Karachi was not merely ethnic or sectarian but was driven by a struggle for control of the city, land grabbing, extortion and criminal activity, and that elements associated with political parties were involved, with many criminals enjoying political patronage. It found that the police had been politicised through appointments on political grounds and that "no-go areas" existed where the state's writ did not run. The Court directed that the police be depoliticised and that appointments and transfers be made on merit, that the Sindh government act against land grabbing, bhatta and armed groups regardless of political affiliation, that no-go areas be eliminated, that illegal weapons be recovered, that a monitoring cell be created, and that cases of targeted killings be investigated and prosecuted effectively. It also recommended that the city not be divided on ethnic or political lines and that delimitation of constituencies be reviewed to reduce ethnic polarisation. The Court kept the matter pending for implementation reports, and later orders in 2012 and 2013 reviewed progress. The judgment is commonly linked to the Karachi operation launched by the Rangers in September 2013.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 184(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (suo motu jurisdiction)
- Article 9, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to life)
- Article 14, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (dignity)
- Article 15, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (freedom of movement)
- Article 24, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (protection of property rights)
- Article 148(3), Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (duty of the federation to protect provinces against internal disturbance)
- Police Order 2002 and Police Act 1861 (as applicable in Sindh; police appointments and transfers)
- Anti-Terrorism Act 1997, Section 6 and Section 7
- Pakistan Rangers Ordinance 1959

## Why It Matters
The Karachi law and order case is the leading example of the Supreme Court using suo motu jurisdiction to address a collapse of public security and to name the political dimension of urban violence. It set out principles on the depoliticisation of the police and on the state's positive duty to protect life, and its findings were relied upon in later orders and in the 2013 Karachi operation. The case also features in debates about the proper limits of judicial intervention in executive and policing matters.

2011 mein Karachi mein target killing, bhatta khori aur zameenon par qabzon ki wajah se sainkron log qatal huay. Chief Justice Iftikhar Chaudhry ne khud notice liya aur Supreme Court ne Karachi mein baith kar hukumat, police, Rangers aur siyasi jamaaton ko suna. Court ne kaha ke Karachi ki larai dar-asal shehar par qabze ki larai hai jis mein siyasi jamaaton se jure log shamil hain aur police siyasi bharti ki wajah se kamzor ho chuki hai. Court ne police ko siyasat se pak karne, no-go areas khatam karne aur mujrimon ke khilaf baghair imtiyaz karwai karne ka hukm diya.
