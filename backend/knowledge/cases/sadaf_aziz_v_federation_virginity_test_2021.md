# Sadaf Aziz v. Federation of Pakistan (Two-Finger / Virginity Test Case)

**Court:** Lahore High Court **Year:** 2021 (judgment of 4 January 2021) **Citation:** PLD 2021 Lahore 300

## Background / Facts
For decades, medico-legal examinations of rape survivors in Pakistan routinely included the "two-finger test" (TFT) and the "hymen test", in which the examining doctor inserted fingers into the vagina to assess the laxity of the vaginal muscles and the state of the hymen, and then recorded an opinion about whether the woman was "habituated to sexual intercourse" or a "virgin". Such findings were then used in trials to attack the character and credibility of the survivor, even though the law had been amended to bar evidence of the victim's past sexual conduct. Sadaf Aziz, a women's rights activist, and other petitioners, including lawyers and civil society members, filed a public interest petition under Article 199 in the Lahore High Court in 2020, challenging the use of these tests in Punjab as unscientific, degrading and contrary to the Constitution. The petition was heard by Justice Ayesha A. Malik, who later became the first woman judge of the Supreme Court of Pakistan. The Punjab government informed the Court that it had already issued guidelines discouraging the tests, but the petitioners pointed out that they continued in practice.

## Legal Issues
- Do the two-finger test and the hymen test have any scientific or forensic value in determining whether rape has occurred?
- Does subjecting a rape survivor to these tests violate her rights to dignity (Article 14), life (Article 9) and privacy, and the right to equal protection (Article 25)?
- Can observations about a survivor's prior sexual activity lawfully be used in a rape trial after the amendments to Article 151(4) of the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984?

## Decision
The Lahore High Court declared the two-finger test and the hymen test illegal and unconstitutional. The Court held that the tests have no scientific basis for proving rape, since the state of the hymen or vaginal laxity cannot establish whether sexual intercourse was consensual or forced, and that their only purpose in practice was to cast doubt on the survivor's character. It held that the tests violate the survivor's dignity under Article 14, her right to life and privacy under Article 9, and the guarantee of equality under Article 25, and amount to a form of re-victimisation that discourages reporting. The Court directed the Punjab government and health authorities to ensure that these tests are not carried out, to revise medico-legal protocols and training in line with the World Health Organization guidelines, and to ensure that medico-legal reports do not record findings about "virginity" or "habituation to sex". The Court noted that the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 2016 and the 2020 anti-rape ordinance had already barred evidence of the survivor's sexual history. The Sindh High Court delivered a similar judgment in February 2021, and the Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021 later prohibited the two-finger test by statute.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 9, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to life, including privacy and bodily integrity)
- Article 14, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (dignity of man and prohibition of degrading treatment)
- Article 25, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (equality of citizens; no discrimination on the basis of sex)
- Article 199, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (High Court writ jurisdiction)
- Article 151(4), Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984 (as amended in 2016, prohibiting evidence of the victim's general immoral character in rape cases)
- Section 376, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (rape)
- Section 164-A, Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (medical examination of the victim of rape)
- Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021, Section 11 (prohibition of the two-finger virginity test), enacted after the judgment

## Why It Matters
The judgment ended a practice that had humiliated rape survivors in Pakistan for decades and had allowed trials to focus on the survivor's character rather than the accused's conduct. It is the leading Pakistani decision on the dignity and privacy rights of survivors in the criminal process and was followed by the Sindh High Court and then by Parliament in the Anti-Rape (Investigation and Trial) Act 2021. It is also cited internationally as part of a wider movement, including the Indian Supreme Court's rulings, to abolish virginity testing.

Pakistan mein ziyadati ka shikar aurton ka medical muaina karte waqt "two-finger test" kiya jata tha aur report mein likha jata tha ke aurat "aadi" hai ya "kunwari" hai, jo phir adalat mein us ke kirdar par hamla karne ke liye istemal hota tha. Sadaf Aziz aur doosron ne Lahore High Court mein is ko challenge kiya. Justice Ayesha Malik ne January 2021 mein faisla diya ke is test ki koi scientific bunyad nahi, yeh aurat ki izzat aur privacy ke khilaf hai, aur yeh ghair qanooni aur ghair aaini hai. Baad mein Sindh High Court ne bhi yahi faisla diya aur 2021 ke anti-rape qanoon mein is test par pabandi laga di gayi.
