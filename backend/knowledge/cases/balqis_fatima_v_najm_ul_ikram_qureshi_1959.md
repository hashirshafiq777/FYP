# Mst. Balqis Fatima v. Najm-ul-Ikram Qureshi

**Court:** West Pakistan High Court, Lahore (now Lahore High Court), full bench **Year:** 1959 **Citation:** PLD 1959 (W.P.) Lahore 566

## Background / Facts
Balqis Fatima was married to Najm-ul-Ikram Qureshi. Relations between the spouses became so bad that she refused to live with him and sought dissolution of the marriage. She could not prove any of the specific grounds listed in the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939, such as cruelty or failure to maintain, but she stated that she had developed such hatred for her husband that living with him was impossible, and she offered to return the dower. The husband opposed dissolution. At that time the prevailing view, based on older Anglo-Muhammadan law texts, was that khula was a contract requiring the husband's agreement, so a court could not impose it. The matter came before a full bench of the Lahore High Court because of its importance.

## Legal Issues
- Is khula a purely consensual transaction between husband and wife, or can the court dissolve a marriage by khula on the wife's suit even if the husband refuses?
- What must a wife show to obtain khula from the court, and must she prove fault on the part of the husband?
- What is the role of the court as qazi under Islamic law in such disputes?

## Decision
The full bench, in a judgment written by Justice B. Z. Kaikaus, held that under Islamic law the wife is entitled to khula through the court when the judge is satisfied that the spouses cannot live together within the limits of Allah, and that the husband's consent is not required. The Court examined the Quranic verse 2:229 and the hadith concerning Jamila, wife of Thabit bin Qais, and concluded that the Prophet (peace be upon him) had ordered dissolution without regard to the husband's wishes. It reasoned that marriage in Islam is not meant to be a prison for the wife and that the qazi's power to dissolve exists precisely for cases where the husband refuses. The Court held that the wife must return the dower or other benefit received, as the court considers fair. Balqis Fatima was granted dissolution of marriage.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939, Section 2 (grounds for dissolution; residuary ground of dissolution under Muslim law)
- Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:229 (basis of khula), applied as Muslim personal law
- Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937, Section 2 (Muslim personal law applies to marriage and dissolution)

## Why It Matters
Balqis Fatima was the first clear judicial recognition in Pakistan that a court can grant khula without the husband's consent, overturning the older view that khula was purely contractual. Its reasoning was adopted and confirmed by the Supreme Court in Khurshid Bibi v. Muhammad Amin (1967), and together the two cases form the basis of the law of khula in Pakistan. The judgment is also significant for its method, going directly to the Quran and Sunnah rather than relying only on classical texts.

## Simple Explanation (Roman Urdu)
Balqis Fatima apne shohar se itni nafrat karne lagi thin ke un ke saath rehna na-mumkin tha, lekin shohar talaq nahi de raha tha aur purane qanoon ke mutabiq khula ke liye shohar ki razamandi zaroori samjhi jati thi. Lahore High Court ke full bench ne 1959 mein Quran aur Hadith ko dekh kar kaha ke agar adalat mutmain ho jaye ke mian biwi Allah ki hadood mein reh kar saath nahi reh sakte to adalat shohar ki marzi ke baghair bhi khula de sakti hai. Aurat ko bas mahr wapas karna hota hai. Yeh faisla baad mein Supreme Court ne Khurshid Bibi case mein barqarar rakha.
