# Muhammad Ismail Qureshi v. Pakistan through Secretary, Law and Parliamentary Affairs

**Court:** Federal Shariat Court **Year:** 1990 (judgment of 30 October 1990, reported in 1991) **Citation:** PLD 1991 FSC 10

## Background / Facts
Section 295-C was inserted into the Pakistan Penal Code in 1986 during the rule of General Zia-ul-Haq. It made the use of derogatory remarks, by words, writing, visible representation or any imputation or insinuation, in respect of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) an offence punishable with "death, or imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to fine". Muhammad Ismail Qureshi, an advocate, filed a Shariat petition in the Federal Shariat Court under Article 203D of the Constitution, arguing that the alternative punishment of life imprisonment was repugnant to the injunctions of Islam, because under Islamic law the only punishment for insulting the Prophet is death. The Federal Government opposed the petition and defended the discretion given to the courts. The Court heard several religious scholars (jurisconsults) before deciding.

## Legal Issues
- Is the punishment for contempt of the Holy Prophet under Islamic law fixed as death, or may a lesser punishment be awarded?
- Is the provision of life imprisonment as an alternative sentence in Section 295-C repugnant to the injunctions of Islam within the meaning of Article 203D?
- Should the section also cover contempt of other Prophets?

## Decision
The Federal Shariat Court held that under the Quran and Sunnah the penalty for contempt of the Holy Prophet is death and that no lesser punishment is permissible, so the words "or imprisonment for life" in Section 295-C were repugnant to the injunctions of Islam. The Court directed the President to take steps to amend the section by removing the alternative sentence, and declared that if the amendment was not made by 30 April 1991 the words would cease to have effect. The Court also observed that the definition should extend to contempt of other Prophets, though this direction was not implemented. The Federal Government filed an appeal to the Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court but later withdrew it, so the judgment became final and death became the only sentence for an offence under Section 295-C. The section's text in the statute book still contains the words "or imprisonment for life", but courts treat them as inoperative.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 295-C, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (derogatory remarks in respect of the Holy Prophet)
- Article 203D, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (power of the Federal Shariat Court to examine whether a law is repugnant to the injunctions of Islam)
- Article 203F, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (appeal to the Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court)
- Criminal Law (Amendment) Act 1986 (which inserted Section 295-C)

## Why It Matters
This decision is the reason Section 295-C carries a mandatory death sentence in practice, which makes it one of the most severe blasphemy provisions in the world and removes judicial discretion in sentencing. It is central to every discussion of blasphemy law reform in Pakistan and to the international criticism of the law. It also shows the power of the Federal Shariat Court under Article 203D to require Parliament to change laws found to be repugnant to Islam.

## Simple Explanation (Roman Urdu)
1986 mein qanoon bana ke Hazrat Muhammad (SAW) ki shan mein gustakhi ki saza maut ya umar qaid hogi. Muhammad Ismail Qureshi naami wakeel ne Federal Shariat Court mein kaha ke Islam mein is jurm ki saza sirf maut hai, umar qaid ka option Islam ke khilaf hai. Federal Shariat Court ne 1990 mein yeh baat maan li aur hukumat ko qanoon se umar qaid wale alfaz nikalne ka hukm diya. Hukumat ne appeal wapas le li, is liye ab Section 295-C mein amalan sirf sazaye maut hai.
