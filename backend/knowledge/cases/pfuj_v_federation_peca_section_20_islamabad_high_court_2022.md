# Pakistan Federal Union of Journalists v. Federation of Pakistan (PECA Section 20 Criminal Defamation Case)

**Court:** Islamabad High Court **Year:** 2022 (judgment of 8 April 2022) **Citation:** citation not confirmed

## Background / Facts
Section 20 of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 (PECA) made it an offence to intentionally and publicly exhibit, display or transmit information through an information system that is known to be false and that intimidates or harms the reputation or privacy of a natural person, punishable with imprisonment of up to three years and fine. Journalists, activists and opposition politicians complained that the Federal Investigation Agency (FIA) used Section 20 to register cases and arrest critics of the government and state institutions. On 18 February 2022, the President promulgated the Prevention of Electronic Crimes (Amendment) Ordinance 2022, which expanded Section 20 to cover defamation of institutions and public office holders (not only natural persons), increased the maximum punishment to five years, made the offence cognizable and non-bailable, allowed any person (not only the aggrieved) to complain, and required trials to be completed within six months. The Pakistan Federal Union of Journalists (PFUJ), the Pakistan Bar Council, media bodies and others challenged the Ordinance and Section 20 before the Islamabad High Court under Article 199. Chief Justice Athar Minallah heard the petitions and had earlier passed interim orders restraining the FIA from arresting journalists under the provision.

## Legal Issues
- Was the PECA (Amendment) Ordinance 2022 a valid exercise of the President's power to promulgate ordinances, and was it consistent with the fundamental rights to freedom of speech (Article 19) and the right to information (Article 19A)?
- Is criminal defamation of the kind penalised by Section 20 of PECA, including of institutions and public office holders, a reasonable restriction on free speech under the Constitution?
- Was the FIA's use of Section 20 against journalists and critics an abuse of power?

## Decision
On 8 April 2022 the Islamabad High Court allowed the petitions, declared the PECA (Amendment) Ordinance 2022 unconstitutional and struck down Section 20 of PECA in its entirety as inconsistent with the Constitution, holding that criminalising defamation in that form unreasonably restricted the freedoms of expression and of the press guaranteed by Article 19 and the right of citizens to information under Article 19A. The Court observed that public office holders and institutions must tolerate criticism and have civil remedies under the Defamation Ordinance 2002, and that the criminal law should not be used to silence dissent. The Court also directed that the Federal Government conduct an inquiry into the FIA cyber crime wing's abuse of its powers in registering cases against journalists and critics, and that the Secretary of the Interior Division fix responsibility. The Federal Government filed an intra-court appeal and the matter went to the Supreme Court; the final outcome of those appeals is not confirmed in this note. In January 2025 Parliament enacted the Prevention of Electronic Crimes (Amendment) Act 2025, which introduced new provisions on "false and fake information" and new regulatory bodies; those provisions were in turn challenged in the High Courts.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Section 20, Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 (offences against dignity of a natural person; criminal defamation online)
- Prevention of Electronic Crimes (Amendment) Ordinance 2022
- Article 19, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (freedom of speech, expression and press)
- Article 19A, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to information)
- Article 89, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (ordinance-making power of the President)
- Article 199, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (High Court writ jurisdiction)
- Defamation Ordinance 2002 (civil remedy for defamation)
- Section 499 and Section 500, Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (criminal defamation, referred to by way of comparison)

## Why It Matters
This judgment is the leading Pakistani decision on online criminal defamation and the limits of the state's power to police speech on the internet. It held that Section 20 of PECA was unconstitutional and set standards for how cyber crime laws must respect Article 19, while also calling out the abuse of FIA powers against journalists. Although later legislation and appeals have kept the area in flux, the judgment is cited by courts and digital rights groups whenever PECA is used against critics, and it is essential context for the 2025 PECA amendments.
