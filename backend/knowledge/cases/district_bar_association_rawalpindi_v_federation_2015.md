# District Bar Association, Rawalpindi v. Federation of Pakistan (18th and 21st Amendment Case)

**Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan **Year:** 2015 **Citation:** PLD 2015 SC 401

## Background / Facts
The Eighteenth Amendment (2010) made wide-ranging changes to the Constitution, including the creation of a Judicial Commission and a Parliamentary Committee for the appointment of judges under the new Article 175A. Bar associations challenged the new appointment process as a threat to judicial independence. After the attack on the Army Public School in Peshawar on 16 December 2014, Parliament passed the Twenty-first Amendment and the Pakistan Army (Amendment) Act 2015, which allowed military courts to try civilians accused of terrorism linked to religion or sects for a period of two years. The Lahore High Court Bar Association, the District Bar Association Rawalpindi, the Supreme Court Bar Association and others challenged both amendments, arguing that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution is limited by its basic structure and that military courts for civilians violate the right to a fair trial. A full court of seventeen judges headed by Chief Justice Nasir-ul-Mulk heard the petitions.

## Legal Issues
- Does the Supreme Court have the power to strike down a constitutional amendment, and is Parliament's amending power under Article 239 subject to any limits?
- Does the Constitution have a "basic structure" or "salient features" that cannot be amended?
- Were the Eighteenth Amendment's provisions on judicial appointments (Article 175A) and the Twenty-first Amendment on military courts valid?

## Decision
On 5 August 2015 the Court, by majority, dismissed the petitions and upheld both amendments. A majority of the judges accepted that the Constitution has salient features, such as democracy, federalism, parliamentary form of government and the independence of the judiciary, and that the Court could examine whether an amendment destroys them; a minority held that there is no judicially enforceable limit on the amending power. On the facts, the majority held that Article 175A, as modified by the Nineteenth Amendment, did not destroy judicial independence. The Twenty-first Amendment was upheld by majority as a temporary measure in an extraordinary situation, with the condition that decisions of military courts remained subject to judicial review by the High Courts and Supreme Court on grounds such as lack of jurisdiction, mala fides or absence of evidence. Several judges dissented and would have struck down the Twenty-first Amendment. The judgment runs to several hundred pages with multiple separate opinions.

## Laws and Provisions Involved
- Article 239, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (constitutional amendment procedure)
- Article 175A, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (Judicial Commission and Parliamentary Committee)
- Article 10A, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (right to fair trial)
- Article 8, Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (laws inconsistent with fundamental rights void)
- Constitution (Eighteenth Amendment) Act 2010
- Constitution (Twenty-first Amendment) Act 2015
- Pakistan Army (Amendment) Act 2015
- Section 2(1)(d), Pakistan Army Act 1952 (persons subject to the Act)

## Why It Matters
This is the leading Pakistani authority on whether constitutional amendments can be reviewed by courts. A majority of the Supreme Court accepted a version of the basic structure doctrine, which the Court had earlier refused in cases such as Pakistan Lawyers Forum v. Federation (2005). The judgment also defined the limited scope of judicial review over military court convictions, which was applied in later cases such as Said Zaman Khan v. Federation (2017). The question of military trials of civilians returned to the Court from 2023 onward after the 9 May 2023 riots.

Peshawar Army Public School par hamle ke baad Parliament ne 21st Amendment ke zariye dehshatgardi ke muqadmaat fauji adalaton mein chalane ki ijazat di. Wukala ne is aur 18th Amendment ko Supreme Court mein challenge kiya. 2015 mein satrah judges ke full court ne aksariyat se dono tarmeemen barqarar rakhin, lekin kaha ke aain ki kuch bunyadi khususiyat hain jinhen Parliament bhi khatam nahi kar sakti, aur fauji adalaton ke faisle bhi aala adalaton mein review ho sakte hain.
